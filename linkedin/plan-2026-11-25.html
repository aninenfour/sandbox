How much of crypto is traded by machines?

LinkedIn copy
About two thirds of on-chain crypto trades are made by bots.
They come from just 1 to 3% of wallets.

That's the latest estimate from on-chain analytics firm Birdeye. Here's what the number measures, and what it doesn't:
→ It counts trades, not trading volume
→ It's on-chain data, led by Solana, where automated activity reaches up to 80% of volume
→ Bots are flagged by behaviour no human can do: several trades per second, buys within seconds of a listing
→ The full methodology is due in December, so treat it as an estimate

What it means if you trade by hand:
→ You're rarely the fastest. Don't compete on speed.
→ Compete on discipline: position size, stops, time horizon.
→ Or automate the boring part: a trading bot follows the rules you set, every time.

Most people see one screen of a crypto exchange. Behind it are at least 8 teams.

The 1% rule in 80 seconds

LinkedIn copy
Most traders blow up on size, not on being wrong.

The 1% rule in one line: risk at most 1% of your account on a single trade.

→ Account: 10,000 USDT, so max risk is 100 USDT
→ Stop: 5% below entry
→ Position size: 100 ÷ 5% = 2,000 USDT
→ Wrong? You lose 100. Ten losses in a row still leave about 90% of the account.

The small number that decides who pays whom

LinkedIn copy
Funding rate: the small number on every perpetual futures screen that decides who pays whom.

→ Perpetual futures never expire, so funding keeps their price close to spot
→ Positive funding: longs pay shorts
→ Negative funding: shorts pay longs
→ It's charged on your position size at set times (see the countdown on the contract page)
→ Very high funding usually means a crowded trade

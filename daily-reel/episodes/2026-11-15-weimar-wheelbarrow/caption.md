In 1923, a German worker got paid twice a day and still couldn't afford bread.

In 1914, one dollar bought about 4.2 marks. By November 1923 it bought over 4.2 trillion. The government paid striking Ruhr workers by printing money, wages lost value by lunch, and a loaf of bread hit 200 billion marks. This week in 1923 the Rentenmark arrived, worth one trillion old marks, and prices finally calmed down.

What the Weimar hyperinflation still teaches:
→ Money is only worth what people trust it to be
→ Printing without limit quietly drains savings
→ Spread your savings so one currency can't wipe you out

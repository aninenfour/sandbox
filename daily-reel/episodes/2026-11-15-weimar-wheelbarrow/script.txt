In 1923, a German worker got paid twice a day, and still couldn't afford bread.
In 1914, one dollar bought about four marks.
Nine years later, it bought over four trillion.
So how does money stop being money? Four steps.
Step one. Germany owes huge war reparations, and in 1923, French and Belgian troops occupy the Ruhr.
Step two. Workers strike, and the government pays them by printing more money.
Step three. Prices climb so fast that a morning wage is worth less by lunch, so people sprint to the shops.
Step four. A loaf of bread hits two hundred billion marks, and families burn banknotes for heat.
Then, this week in 1923, the new Rentenmark arrives, worth a trillion old marks, and prices calm down.
When money gets printed without limit, savings quietly disappear.

In 1925, a con man sold the Eiffel Tower. It wasn't his to sell.

Victor Lustig posed as a government official, told Paris scrap dealers the tower was being scrapped in secret, and walked away with a buyer's money and a bribe on top. The buyer was too embarrassed to go to the police.

What the Eiffel Tower con still teaches:
→ "Keep this quiet" is a red flag, not a privilege
→ Urgency is there to stop you from checking
→ Verify who you're paying before you pay

Two pizzas. Ten thousand bitcoin. Here's how that happened.
It's May 2010, and a programmer in Florida named Laszlo Hanyecz has a pile of bitcoin he mined himself.
But back then, nobody actually spends it on anything.
Step one. He posts on a bitcoin forum: ten thousand coins for a couple of large pizzas.
Step two. Four days go by, and nobody bites.
Step three. Then a nineteen-year-old named Jeremy takes the deal and orders two Papa John's pizzas to Laszlo's door.
Step four. Laszlo gets dinner, and bitcoin gets its first famous real-world purchase, worth about forty-one dollars.
Today, at around eighty thousand dollars a coin, those pizzas are worth about eight hundred million.
And Laszlo says he has no regrets.
Because money only becomes money when somebody's brave enough to use it first.

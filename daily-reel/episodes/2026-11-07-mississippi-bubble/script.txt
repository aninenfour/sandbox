The man hired to save France's money ended up breaking it.
John Law, a Scottish gambler who once escaped prison after a deadly duel.
In 1716, France is drowning in war debt, and Law's fix is paper money.
So here's how the Mississippi Bubble happened, in four steps.
Step one. Law opens a Paris bank that swaps gold for paper notes.
Step two. He sells shares in the Mississippi Company, promising New World riches, at five hundred livres each.
Step three. By 1719, crowds pack one Paris street, and shares hit ten thousand. A new word appears: millionaire.
Step four. In 1720, the printing never stops, everyone cashes out, and the shares collapse.
Law flees France and later dies poor in Venice.
Paper riches are only worth what's really behind them.

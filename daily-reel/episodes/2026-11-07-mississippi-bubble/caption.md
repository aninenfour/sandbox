The man hired to save France's money ended up breaking it.

In 1716, Scottish gambler John Law got France to try paper money. His Mississippi Company shares went from 500 livres to about 10,000 by 1719, Paris coined the word "millionaire", and in 1720 it all collapsed. Law fled France and died poor in Venice.

What the Mississippi Bubble still teaches:
→ A price is not the same as value
→ When everyone wants out at once, the exit gets tiny
→ Paper riches are only worth what's really behind them

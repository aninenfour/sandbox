A Vermont janitor died with eight million dollars, and nobody in his town knew.
Ronald Read pumped gas for twenty-five years, then swept floors at J.C. Penney.
He wore old flannel shirts and drove a second-hand Toyota.
So how does a janitor quietly get rich? Four steps.
Step one. He buys boring companies that pay dividends, like Procter and Gamble and Johnson and Johnson.
Step two. Every dividend goes straight back into more shares.
Step three. He holds for decades, and the certificates stack up five inches thick.
Step four. He skips the hot stocks, owns ninety-five companies, and a Lehman Brothers loss barely dents him.
When he dies in 2014, at ninety-two, he leaves six million dollars to his local hospital and library.
You don't need a big salary, you need time.

## LinkedIn

```
Paper money was invented twice, 637 years apart, and both stories end the same way 📜

In 11th century Sichuan, everyday coins were made of iron. Heavy enough that merchants began holding coins on deposit and issuing paper receipts, and people traded the receipts instead of the metal. In 1024 the Song government took over issuance and produced the first official notes.

It worked while issuance was disciplined. Once the state needed money, printing more was the easy option, and each new note was worth a little less. The Yuan and Ming dynasties repeated the pattern, and by the 1500s China had largely returned to silver.

Sweden reached the same idea in the 1600s for the same reason. Its copper plate money was so heavy that the largest pieces weighed almost 20 kg. In 1661 Stockholms Banco issued Europe's first banknotes. By 1664 it had issued more notes than it could redeem in copper, and it failed.

Two separate inventions, the same practical problem, the same ending. The useful takeaway is that paper money was never about paper. It is a technology for trust, and the discipline behind the issuance is what that trust rests on.
```

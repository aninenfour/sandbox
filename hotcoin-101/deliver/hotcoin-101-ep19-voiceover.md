# FILE 019 · A Market That Started Under a Tree

```
In 1792, twenty-four guys in New York met under a tree and, well, accidentally started Wall Street.

The deal was tiny. On the seventeenth of May they signed two promises: we deal with each other first, and nobody charges less than a quarter of a percent. That's the whole Buttonwood Agreement, and look at what it really is, a club with a door and a minimum price.

A year later they moved inside, into a coffee house, and by 1817 the club had a proper name, the New York Stock and Exchange Board.

And that minimum fee? It lasted, uhm, almost two centuries, until regulators ended fixed commissions on the first of May, 1975, and trading got cheaper for everyone.

Today most trades run on servers, and the tree is long gone, but the idea under it never changed.

Every market starts as an agreement about who gets to trade, and at what price.
```

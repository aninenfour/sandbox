## LinkedIn

```
Most traders look at the price. Fewer look at the liquidity behind it, and that's the number that decides what they actually get 💧

Liquidity is how much you can trade without moving the price. It shows up in the order book as depth: buy orders stacked below the current price, sell orders stacked above, and a spread between the best of each. When the stacks are deep, a large order fills close to the quote. When they're thin, the order works through one price level after another and the average fill drifts away from the screen price. That gap is slippage.

The part that surprises people is that liquidity isn't a pool of money. It's a set of resting orders, and the people who place them can cancel them in an instant. Under stress they often do, all at once, which is why liquidity tends to disappear exactly when it's needed most.

The 6 May 2010 Flash Crash is the textbook case. The joint SEC and CFTC staff report on that day describes market participants withdrawing liquidity as prices fell, and in the gap some large, healthy companies printed absurd trades. Accenture traded at $0.01 for a few seconds before recovering.

A practical habit for any market: before a sizeable order, look at the depth and the spread, not just the last price, and use limit orders where slippage matters.
```

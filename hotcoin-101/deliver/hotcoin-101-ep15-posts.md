## LinkedIn

```
Crypto slang sorts the market into a zoo of whales, shrimp and bagholders, and the labels say more about concentration than about wealth 🐋

The animal names come from on-chain analytics. Glassnode, for example, groups holders by balance: shrimp hold under 1 BTC, whales hold 1,000 to 5,000 BTC, and "humpbacks" hold more. A whale matters because a single sale can move the price for everyone else. A bagholder is different: anyone, at any size, who bought near the top and is still holding.

There is an important catch in how these numbers get quoted. A widely shared statistic claimed that 2% of accounts controlled 95% of all bitcoin, but it counted addresses, not people. One exchange address can hold coins on behalf of a very large number of users, and one person can spread holdings across many addresses. Glassnode's own entity-based estimate put the upper bound much lower, at around 71.5% held by 2% of entities, as of January 2021.

The practical point still stands. When a handful of wallets genuinely hold most of a token's supply, one of them selling can push the price down for every smaller holder. Checking the holder distribution before buying is a basic piece of risk management.
```

# FILE 016 · Liquidity: Noticed Only When It Is Gone

```
The screen says a hundred dollars, you hit sell, and you get, well, ninety-four. Nobody robbed you, the buyers just ran out.

Liquidity is how much you can trade without moving the price, and it lives in the order book, buyers stacked on one side, sellers on the other, and the gap between them is the spread.

Deep stacks soak up a big order. Thin ones get eaten level by level, and the price slides as you fill, which is slippage.

And here's the catch, those stacks are just orders, and orders can be cancelled, so when things get scary they vanish, uhm, all at once.

On May 6, 2010, that hit US stocks, and for a moment Accenture traded at one cent.

So check the depth, not just the price, because the price is a quote and the fill is what you actually get.
```

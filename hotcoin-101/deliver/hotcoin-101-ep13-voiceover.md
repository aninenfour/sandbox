# FILE 013 · How Paper Money Got Invented Twice

```
China was printing paper money more than six hundred years before Europe even tried it.

It started with a practical problem. Coins in Sichuan were iron, and iron is... well, heavy.

So merchants kept the coins and handed out paper receipts, and people traded the receipts instead.

In ten twenty-four the government took over and printed the first official notes.

Which worked, until the state needed money, and printing more is easy, so every new note was worth a bit less.

The Mongols did it too, then the Ming, and by the fifteen hundreds China was back on silver.

Then Sweden, copper coins this time, and the biggest one weighed almost twenty kilos.

So in sixteen sixty-one a Stockholm bank printed Europe's first banknotes, and, uhm, three years later it had printed too many and couldn't pay.

Two inventions, same problem, same ending.

Paper money was never really about the paper. It's a technology for trust, and trust is always the scarce part.
```

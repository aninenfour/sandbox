## LinkedIn

```
The New York Stock Exchange traces its origin to a two-sentence agreement signed outdoors 🌳

On 17 May 1792, 24 brokers signed what became known as the Buttonwood Agreement. It did two things: the signatories would give preference to each other in their dealings, and none would charge a commission below one quarter of one percent. In practice, it created a membership with a fixed minimum price, and a reason to route business inside the group rather than through outside auctioneers.

The group moved indoors to the Tontine Coffee House in 1793 and organised formally as the New York Stock & Exchange Board in 1817. The fixed commission structure proved remarkably durable: it lasted until 1 May 1975, when the SEC's abolition of fixed rates ("May Day") opened brokerage to price competition and brought trading costs down sharply.

The technology has changed completely since then. The underlying design question has not: every market, including today's crypto exchanges, is defined by who is allowed to trade on it and on what terms.
```

## LinkedIn

```
A stablecoin is a promise that one coin is worth one dollar. The useful question is not whether the promise is made, but what stands behind it ⚖️

There are three broad designs.

Fiat-backed coins work like a coat check. Users hand over dollars, the issuer holds them in reserve, and each coin can be redeemed one for one. The risk sits in the reserve itself. In March 2023, Circle disclosed that $3.3 billion of USDC's reserves, about 8%, was held at Silicon Valley Bank when it failed. USDC traded below a dollar until the peg was restored four days later.

Over-collateralised coins skip the bank. Borrowers lock up more crypto than they borrow, and if the collateral falls past a set threshold it is sold automatically, before the coin itself is undercovered.

Algorithmic coins rely on a formula rather than reserves. Terra's UST held its peg through a mint-and-burn relationship with its sister token, LUNA. From 9 May 2022 the mechanism failed, UST fell to around 10 cents, and roughly $45 billion of market value disappeared within a week.

The peg is a claim, not a law. Any stablecoin is only as stable as the assets and mechanisms standing behind it, which is worth checking before treating one as cash.
```

# FILE 020 · Gas, Blockspace and Why Moving Costs Money

Facts used on screen and below (checked):
- Ethereum makes a block about every 12 seconds; a plain ETH transfer uses 21,000 gas.
- CryptoKitties, December 2017: the craze took about 10 to 15% of Ethereum traffic, and about 30,000 transactions were stuck waiting (BBC, CoinDesk, TechCrunch).
- Otherside land sale (Yuga Labs), 30 April 2022: buyers spent roughly 55,000 to 64,000 ETH on gas, about $157M to $183M (Decrypt, The Defiant).
- Dencun upgrade, 13 March 2024: median fees on rollups such as Base and Optimism fell to a few cents (DL News, The Defiant).
- Fees today (Nov 2026): Ethereum gas mostly well under 1 gwei through 2026, so a plain L1 transfer usually costs cents; L2s a fraction of a cent. Captions say "usually costs cents" because a rush can still spike it.
- The $50 / $20 open and the gwei bids in the auction scene are labelled illustrative on screen.

## LinkedIn

```
Transaction fees on Ethereum are not a tax. They are the clearing price of a recurring auction ⛽

Roughly every 12 seconds the network produces a block with a fixed capacity, measured in gas. A plain ETH transfer uses 21,000 gas. When demand for that capacity exceeds supply, users bid a price per unit, the block is filled from the highest bids down, and the rest wait.

Two episodes show what happens when demand spikes. In December 2017, CryptoKitties briefly accounted for a meaningful share of all Ethereum traffic and left around 30,000 transactions pending. On 30 April 2022, the Otherside land sale drew so much simultaneous demand that buyers spent an estimated $157M to $183M on gas.

The structural answer has been layer 2 rollups, which batch hundreds of transactions and settle them as one. Since the Dencun upgrade in March 2024, median fees on several rollups have fallen to a few cents.
```

## LinkedIn

The most underrated number in trading isn't the win rate. It's the size of the drawdown you can come back from 📉

Recovery is not symmetric. A 10% loss needs an 11% gain to get back to even, a 50% loss needs 100%, and a 70% loss needs 233%. The formula is simple: the gain needed is 1 ÷ (1 − loss) − 1. The deeper the hole, the faster it outruns any realistic recovery.

Streaks make this worse than intuition suggests. In 100 independent trades with a 50% win rate, the probability of at least one run of five or more consecutive losses is about 81%. Streaks are not bad luck; they are the expected texture of a fair game.

Position size decides what a streak does to you. Risking 2% of the account per trade, five straight losses leave about 90% of the capital. Risking 20% per trade, the same five losses leave about 33%, and the account then needs a 205% gain to break even. Same strategy, same luck, very different outcome.

That is the idea behind "risk of ruin": the first job of any trading plan is to make sure you're still in the game when the edge pays off.

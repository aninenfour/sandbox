# FILE 020 · Gas, Blockspace and Why Moving Costs Money

```
Ever paid more in fees than the thing you were sending? You weren't paying a bank. You were, well, losing an auction.

Here's the setup. On Ethereum a new block leaves about every twelve seconds, and each one only has so much room. Gas is just how much room your transaction takes, and a plain transfer is twenty-one thousand units.

So when more people want in than there's room for, it turns into an auction. You bid a price per unit, the block fills from the highest bid down, and if you bid too low you, uhm, wait for the next one.

That's how a game about breeding cartoon cats, CryptoKitties, jammed the network in 2017, with about thirty thousand transactions stuck in line. And one night in 2022, a single NFT land sale got so crowded that buyers spent well over a hundred and fifty million dollars just on gas.

The way out is layer two. Rollups bundle hundreds of transactions and post them as one, and since an upgrade in 2024, fees on some of them are a few cents.

Fees aren't a tax. They're an auction, and every time you hit send, you're one of the bidders. So check how busy the road is before you pay for the ride.
```

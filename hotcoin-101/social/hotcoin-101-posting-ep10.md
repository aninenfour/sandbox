1/ Three buttons on every exchange, and most losses come from pressing the wrong one 🟢

2/ They all go to the same place. Buyers queue below the price, sellers queue above it. The only thing an order type decides is whether you join that queue or step across it.

3/ Market order. Certainty about filling, none about price. It takes the best price available, then the next one, and keeps going until it's full. In a thin book that can be several percent from where you clicked.

4/ Limit order. The other way round. You name the number and wait for somebody to come to you. Usually cheaper, and sometimes it simply never happens while the move goes without you.

5/ Stop. The one people get wrong. It's a trigger, not a price guarantee. It sits dormant until the market reaches your level, then fires an ordinary order into whatever book exists at that moment.

6/ So what you're choosing:

→ Market: you give up the price, you get the fill
→ Limit: you give up the certainty, you get your number
→ Stop: in a gap you give up both, you get an exit that runs without you

7/ Order type is a risk decision wearing the costume of a convenience setting.

Most trading losses get blamed on the call. A fair share of them are the button 🟢

Every order goes to the same place. Buyers queue below the current price, sellers queue above it, and the only thing an order type decides is whether you join that queue or step across it.

Stop. The one people misread. It's a trigger, not a price guarantee. It sits dormant until the market reaches your level, then fires an ordinary order into whatever book exists at that moment, which in a gap is not the number you had in mind.

So the actual choice is what you give up:

→ Market: the price, in exchange for a fill right now
→ Limit: the certainty, in exchange for your number
→ Stop: in a gap, both, in exchange for an exit that runs without you

Speed, price, certainty. You get two of the three.

## LinkedIn

Takes the 16:9.

```
There are only two ways to hold crypto, and people usually pick one without noticing they made a choice 🔑

The strange part is that you never really hold a coin, because there isn't one. Ownership is a line in a shared ledger, and a private key is simply the thing that lets you move that line. Whoever holds the key holds the asset.

So either you keep that key yourself, and nobody can freeze it, lend it out, or approve your withdrawal. There's also no support line and no password reset, and a lost phrase means the coins sit on the ledger forever, just out of reach.

Or somebody holds it for you, and what you have is a claim rather than a key. That buys resets, deep liquidity and someone to call, and it puts their security and their solvency inside your risk.

Which is the actual trade:

→ Your mistake is final, or their failure is your problem
→ Nobody can freeze it, or somebody can
→ Operational risk, or counterparty risk

Worth knowing too: roughly a fifth of all bitcoin is estimated to be sitting in wallets nobody can open any more. Self custody tends to fail quietly. Custody tends to fail loudly. Both are permanent.

Most people end up splitting it. Trading size stays where they trade, long term size goes somewhere slower, and nothing sits anywhere by accident.

File 009 of Hotcoin 101, a short series on how markets actually work.
```

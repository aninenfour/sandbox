## LinkedIn

Takes the 16:9.

```
Most people think of leverage as a multiplier on returns. It's more useful to think of it as a clock ⏱️

Put $100 of margin into a 20x position and you control $2,000. A 5% move against that position is the entire $100. Not a 5% loss. All of it.

Each step up in leverage pulls the liquidation price closer to entry. Roughly 50% away at 2x, 20% at 5x, 10% at 10x, and around 5% at 20x, before fees and maintenance margin make it tighter still.

That changes the question. Take the same view on the same market at 10x and at 20x. The price dips, recovers, and ends up exactly where the trader expected. At 10x the position rides out the dip. At 20x it was closed during it. Both traders were right. Only one was allowed to be right late.

Funding adds to it. On perpetual futures, funding is charged on the full position, not the margin, so in a crowded market the clock costs money every interval it runs.

The practical version: before adding leverage, know the exact liquidation price, know the holding cost, and know how long the idea realistically needs to play out. Then size for how long you might be wrong, rather than for how confident you feel.

```

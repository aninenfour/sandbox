### Paste block, FILE 007

```
In 1637, a single flower bulb sold for more than a house.

Tulips reached the Netherlands from the Ottoman empire and became a status symbol. The most prized ones were streaked with colour.

Then people stopped trading flowers. Bulbs sit underground most of the year, so traders started buying paper promises on next season's crop.

Prices went vertical. At the peak, one rare bulb was worth years of a craftsman's wages. Then an auction in Haarlem found no buyers.

Within weeks it was over. Nearly four hundred years ago.

Every one since has borrowed the same three things. Something new nobody can price, paper that moves faster than the asset, and a story.

Swap the bulbs for whatever is running today, and the shape of the thing is identical.

Bubbles are never really about the asset. They are about the story that makes the price sound reasonable.
```
